# Handheld Video Motion Sickness Experiment

## Experimental Setup
To conduct this experiment, I found an unstabilized point-of-view (POV) video of someone fast-walking through a busy city street. I sat unusually close to my monitor (about 20 cm away) to ensure the screen filled my entire field of view, put the video on full screen, and endured it for exactly two minutes. I then had two friends sit in the exact same position and do the same.

Here are the notes on what we felt.

## Testimony 1: My Experience
Almost immediately, I noticed my eyes straining, maybe because I brought the screen too close to my face. The camera was aggressively moving up and down with every footstep, my eyes were constantly darting around trying to find a stable point to focus on, which was exhausting. By the one minute time into the experiment, I started felling some slight head ache, accompanied by a slight, uneasy feeling in my stomach. When the two minutes were finally over and I looked away, the stationary room around me seemed to "swim" or shift for a few seconds before my brain stabilized again. 

## Testimony 2: Big boy about 17 years old
He seemed to be the most affected by the experiment. He reported: 
> "I hated this right from the start, because the screen was so close. After about 90 seconds, I felt nausea and started sweating slightly. I actually had to close my eyes for the last ten seconds because I felt like vimitting."

## Testimony 3: Adult girl about 21 years old
This girl used to play a lot of Ndorchi, so her reaction was slightly different. She reported:
> "I didn't get sick to my stomach like you, but I felt incredibly off-balance. I felt dizzy when I stood up out of the chair."

## The VR Connection
As you noted, this exercise is a perfect, weakened simulation of a bad Virtual Reality experience. It demonstrates **sensory conflict** (or visual-vestibular mismatch). When your eyes tell your brain that you are moving erratically, but your inner ear (vestibular system) tells your brain that you are completely stationary, the body reacts defensively with dizziness, eye strain, and nausea (cybersickness).
